# Conceptual Framework

## Core concept
**Communicative access**: the practical ability of multilingual publics to receive, interpret, verify, and act on public-benefit information across institutional and technological channels.

## Primary theoretical pillars
- Communication Accommodation Theory
- Co-Cultural Theory
- Administrative Burden

## Supporting frameworks
- Communicative (Dis)enfranchisement
- Deservingness and boundary rhetoric
- Framing, threat construction, and fraud amplification

## Working construct
**Communicative administrative burden**: the additional cognitive, linguistic, technological, and verification labor required to understand and act on public information.

## AI governance question
**Who translates the state, and who checks the translation?**

## Conceptual flow
Political/cultural discourse → institutional language-access regime → digital/AI mediation → institutional accommodation → communicative access/burden → outcomes.

## Current pause point
Conceptual development is paused. The next workstream is data infrastructure, multilingual corpus construction, and eventual Amharic–Afaan Oromo model development.
